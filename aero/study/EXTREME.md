## The search

2700 designs on a coarse grid (span 560-840 mm, root chord 82-104 mm, taper 0.5-1.0, tail arm 240-420 mm) and 1125 on a fine one round the best. 1627 could be built and were flown; 208 passed. Dropped:

- no spar fits the tip: 1732
- more power at 13 m/s than the single boom: 1419
- the SG90's tabs do not fit between the spars: 406
- no boom keeps the tail as stiff as the single boom's: 37
- fin taller than the bed: 20
- the wing does not balance over the fuselage: 3

## Washout

The search flies every planform with 2° of twist on the outer panels. The chosen one, at its weight, with the twist from 0 to 4° (tip nose-down), and where along the half span the first section reaches its maximum lift (the outer panels start at 189 mm):

| 2.5° | 0.962 | 8.39 | 0 mm (inboard) | 11.63 | 8.97 |

| 4° | 0.948 | 8.45 | 0 mm (inboard) | 11.62 | 9.02 |

Even untwisted, this planform reaches its maximum lift first at the root (the inner panels keep the full chord, and the tips fly at a lower Reynolds number, where the section's maximum lift is lower), as the single boom's straight wing does. So the twist is not needed for a root-first stall here; it unloads the tips, which costs a little maximum lift and gains a little glide. The design keeps the twist that glides best while the stall still starts inboard of the ailerons, so they keep working: 3°.

## The lifting line against a vortex lattice

Both wings alone in AeroSandbox's vortex lattice (24 panels across each half, 8 along the chord, the twist included), at each plane's lift coefficient at 13 m/s and at its best glide. Both methods are inviscid, so the induced drag compares like for like:

| | lift coefficient | induced drag, lifting line | vortex lattice | span efficiency, lifting line | vortex lattice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single boom, 13 m/s | 0.458 | 0.01387 | 0.01368 | 0.963 | 0.976 |
| Single boom, best glide | 0.516 | 0.01759 | 0.01735 | 0.963 | 0.976 |
| Extreme aero, 13 m/s | 0.400 | 0.00601 | 0.00606 | 0.995 | 0.986 |
| Extreme aero, best glide | 0.644 | 0.01558 | 0.01579 | 0.994 | 0.980 |

The design's induced drag scales these by the same factor for both planes (the 0.8 span efficiency it takes for the straight wing with the fuselage), so what matters is the ratio between them. At 13 m/s the extreme wing has 0.434 of the single boom's induced drag on the lifting line and 0.443 on the vortex lattice: the lifting line is 2 % kinder on the extreme wing, which is 0.0010 N, 0.4 % of its drag.

## Method and limits

- Span loading: Glauert's lifting line, 40 terms, the section's own lift slope (capped at 2π), with the twist. Its span efficiency scales the design's 0.8 at the rectangular AR 5 wing (factor 0.831), so the fuselage's and the viscous share stay as before.
- Profile drag: NeuralFoil on kipina-opt at each of 40 stations' own lift coefficient and Reynolds number (n_crit 7.0).
- Maximum lift: the single boom's 0.967, scaled by the lift the planform carries when its first section reaches its maximum, against the rectangular wing's.
- Everything else: the single boom's drag build-up (aero/models/airframe.py), tail plates and boom re-sized.
- Spar: the lightest carbon tube that keeps the 1 g bending under 1.5 % of the half span (E 70 GPa, even load) and fits the tip's depth with both skins.
- Boom: the lightest roll-wrapped tube whose first bending mode, with the tail, motor and prop on its end, is no lower than the single boom's 46 Hz.
- Weight: the single boom's CAD weight, with each re-sized part at the CAD's own grams per mm² or per mm. `build.py --variant extreme` then builds it and weighs the real parts.
- Handbook uncertainty is the same as the drag optimizer's (perhaps ±30 % on absolute drag); the differences between the two planes are more trustworthy than either number.
