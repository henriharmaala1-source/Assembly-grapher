    The rods

    The carbon boom and spars are printed too, solid, lying on a small flat. The holes are drilled again for them plus the fit clearance: a scaled hole would be the scaled rod plus half the full-size clearance, too tight to print. The spars are as thick as the wing allows with 0.8 mm of wall round the hole, and each is two halves that meet inside the wing centre, so they fit the bed.

    How it goes together

    1. Glue the two fuselage halves. Slide the boom into its socket and glue it, then the motor mount on its end, the motor dummy on the mount and the prop.
    2. Slide the tail mount on the boom; glue the stabiliser under it and the fin into its socket. Glue the elevator and the rudder at neutral.
    3. Drop the servo, ESC, flight controller, receiver, VTX and camera dummies into their places and the battery into its bay. Put on the lid and push the antenna through it.
    4. Push the main and rear spars through the wing centre and both panels. Put the aileron servo dummies in their pockets, glue the fairings over them and the ailerons on.
    5. Seat the wing on the fuselage and stand the plane in the stand's cradle.

    It shows every printed part's fit against its neighbours, the spar and boom paths, the pockets and bays for the bought parts, and the plane's look. It can't show the wiring (left off), the pushrods' travel, the full-size print tolerances or the strength.
