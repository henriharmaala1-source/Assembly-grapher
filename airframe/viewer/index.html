Kipinä 450

A 450 mm fixed wing built around two plain 6 mm tubes. They carry the pod, the wing and an H-tail with twin rudders; every other part prints on a Bambu Lab A1 mini. Four SG90 servos give roll, pitch and yaw, and 450 mm is the smallest span that still hand-launches below 9 m/s with them and an analog FPV kit.

      Why 430 mm

      A printed plane does not shrink evenly. The electronics weigh the same at any size, and a printed skin cannot get thinner than one nozzle line, so the wing loading climbs as the span drops.

      The sweep rebuilds the layout at each span, re-balances it and estimates the weight. 440 mm is the first span that stalls below the 9 m/s hand-launch limit and still fits an SG90's mounting tabs between the wing spars. The build rounds up by 10 mm, and the CAD weights confirm it passes.

      Yaw control costs about 11 g: a fourth SG90, two printed rudders and the linkage. Without rudders the same sweep lands at 420 mm.

      Balance

      Distances from the motor mounting face. The battery slides in the pod to set the CG; the strap slots allow the travel shown.

      Bill of process

      One builder and one A1 mini. Print times come from PrusaSlicer on an A1-mini-like profile; hand times are Boothroyd–Dewhurst handling and insertion estimates plus shop times for gluing, soldering and bending. Press Assemble on the model to watch the same sequence.

      DFMA

      Design efficiency is 3 s × the theoretical minimum part count, divided by the actual assembly time. A part counts toward the minimum when it moves relative to the parts already assembled, must be a different material, or must come apart for assembly or service.

      Also needed
