# Hunting an 8 mm-arm Mark4 10-inch frame

No mainstream catalogue Mark4 (MK4) 10-inch frame ships with 8 mm carbon arms. Across GEPRC, Readytosky, RJX, Tarot, Aocoda, FlyfishRC, UDC, Axisflying-branded and dozens of marketplace clones, the 10-inch Mark4 is sold with **7 mm** arms (GEPRC original, 428–429 mm) or **7.5 mm** arms (the 427 mm "Mark4 V2" clone family), with some 6 mm and 5 mm cuts. Only two leads pair the Mark4 name with an 8 mm figure, and neither is a clean match. One is a doubtful Czech shop listing for a "Mark4 V2 10 inch 8mm arm". The other is a Ukrainian pair of 8 mm replacement arms that are **fiberglass, not carbon**. If you need a confirmed 8 mm carbon arm in this class, it comes from non-Mark4 frames: the **HGLRC Rekon10 Pro V2** (474 mm, 8 mm on HGLRC's own page), the older **Rekon10 Pro V1** (455 mm) and the **iFlight Helion 10** (418 mm, 8 mm on iFlight's own page). The **iFlight XL10 V6** is also listed as 8 mm, but only by resellers, and iFlight says 7 mm. For the frame you are reverse-engineering, the standoff spacings (a 20 mm second pair, a rear pair of about 19–20 mm and a much wider front pair) match the **GEPRC Mark4 geometry**, not the 427 mm V2 clone, which uses 24 mm pairs. The roughly 24 mm inner arm-hole spacing does not match either reference, which suggests a GEPRC-outline clone or a custom arm. **Caveat on the data:** every page fetch in this research was blocked by the session's network proxy, so all figures come from search-engine summaries of the cited pages. Check each one in a browser before you buy or cut.

## The Mark4 10-inch family tops out at 7.5 mm

The original **GEPRC GEP-Mark4-10** lists a **7 mm arm plate plus a 3 mm vertical "arm bar"**. Its other specs are a 428 mm motor-to-motor distance, a 2.5 mm side plate, 3 mm base, 2 mm top, 3 mm mid plate and about 270 g ([GetFPV](https://www.getfpv.com/geprc-mark4-10-frame-kit.html)). Some listings give 429 mm ([rcdrone.top](https://rcdrone.top/products/geprc-gep-mark4-10-429mm-wheelbase-10-inch-fpv-drone-frame)). Several resellers repeat a "3.7mm Thickness Arm Structure" bullet. That figure is almost certainly a copy error for 7 mm + 3 mm, because the same spec lists give the 7 mm plate and the 3 mm bar separately ([Team BlackSheep](https://www.team-blacksheep.com/products/product:7652)). ReadyMadeRC had it in stock at **$66.43** ([RMRC](https://www.readymaderc.com/products/details/86588-geprc-mark4-10-multirotor-frame)). Pyrodrone lists it at $76.99, out of stock, with a 376 × 303 mm footprint ([Pyrodrone](https://pyrodrone.com/products/geprc-gep-mark4-10-fpv-10-long-range-frame-kit)). Its hole patterns are FC 30.5 × 30.5 M3, VTX 30.5/25.5/20, camera 19 mm and motors 19 × 19 M3 ([GEPRC](https://geprc.com/product/gep-mark4-frame/)), and it uses **30 mm aluminium standoffs** ([UAV Garage](https://uavgarage.com/shop/geprc-gep-mark4-10-frame-kit-for-fpv-drone/)). The GEPRC Mark4 LR10 BNF also lists 7 mm arms with a 3 mm brace ([Rotorama](https://www.rotorama.com/product/geprc-geprc-mark4-lr10-6s-elrs-915mhz)). GEPRC sells 10-inch replacement arms at **€7.79 each** ([Rotorama](https://www.rotorama.com/product/geprc-mark4-dily)).

The second family is the **"Mark4 V2 10-inch" at 427 mm**, with an outline of about **268 × 332 mm**. It is one factory-style cut sold under many labels, and it is the thickest catalogue Mark4. Readytosky lists "Arm thickness: 7.5mm", a 2 mm top, a 3 mm bottom and 270 g. The same page gives both 427 mm and 473 mm, which looks like a digit swap ([Rotorama](https://www.rotorama.com/product/readytosky-mark4-10-v2)). Tarot's own Chinese page for the MARK4-10 TL1613 says **"机臂厚度：7.5mm"** (arm thickness 7.5 mm), 427 mm, 238 g, 3 mm top and bottom ([Tarot](http://tarotrc.com/Product/Detail.aspx?Lang=zh-cn&Id=56801373-f309-4c85-b175-436961af57b2)). A UK reseller lists the same Tarot frame at 7 mm ([Flying Tech](https://www.flyingtech.co.uk/product/tarot-10-inch-drone-frame-tl1613/)); the maker's figure is the one to trust. The AliExpress multi-size listing ties thickness to size, "10inch 427mm w/ 7.5mm Arm", with 6 mm arms for the 8- and 9-inch versions ([AliExpress](https://www.aliexpress.com/item/1005005897270664.html)). Other V2 listings give 7.5 mm (RJX via [rcdrone.top](https://rcdrone.top/ja/products/rjxhobby-mark4-v2-10-inch-fpv-frame-kit-ja), an Axisflying-labelled "3K MARK4 V2 10" via [copterparts.ru](https://copterparts.ru/product/%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0-3k-mark4-v2-10/)) and a "7.5mm Carbon Beast" at [Unmanned Tech](https://www.unmannedtechshop.co.uk/de/products/mark4-10-v2-long-range-fpv-frame-7-5mm-carbon-beast). Some sellers drop to 7 mm (Zbotic, 424 mm, [Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/product/mark4-v2-10-inch-carbon-fiber-fpv-frame-424mm-wheelbase/)), 6 mm ([Anubis RC](https://anubisrc.com/product/mark4-10inch-v2-frame-kit-t700-toray-cf/), [eBay at $34.57](https://www.ebay.com/itm/395595061304)) or 5 mm (450 mm, [The FPV Project](https://thefpvproject.com/product/mark10-v2-10-inch-carbon-fiber-fpv-frame/)). The V2's published hole patterns are FC 30.5 or 20 × 20, motors 16 × 16 or 19 × 19, a 19 mm camera, and **35 mm internal height on 8 × M3 × 35 × Ø6 standoffs** ([Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/product/mark4-v2-10-inch-carbon-fiber-fpv-frame-424mm-wheelbase/); [fpvdroneframe.com](https://fpvdroneframe.com/product/mark4-v2-10-inch-long-range-fpv-frame/)).

Other Mark4-branded 10-inch frames do not change the picture. Aocoda lists "Mark 4 10inch 420mm Arm Thickness 7mm" ([Aocoda-RC](https://www.aocoda-rc.com/products/72)) and FlyfishRC lists 7 mm ([Rotorama](https://www.rotorama.com/product/flyfish-mark4-10)). The Ukrainian **UDC Mark4 10** has 7 mm 3K arms and a roughly 305 × 370 mm outline, close to GEPRC's ([UDC](https://www.udc.net.ua/en/products/udc-mark4-10)). FIBERFORM advertises "reinforced" 7 mm arms ([alexandron.shop](https://alexandron.shop/ukrainska-rama-dlia-drona-marko4-10-diuimiv-fiberform-mark4-10/)). No listing found offers a selectable 7/8 mm arm on a 10-inch Mark4. Variant menus choose the frame size, and the arm thickness follows from the size. Within the Mark4 name, 8 mm carbon arms appear only on the larger **Readytosky Mark4 V3 Pro 13-inch (570 mm)** and **15-inch (680 mm)** frames ([Rotorama 13"](https://rotorama.com/product/mark4-v3-pro-13); [Rotorama 15"](https://www.rotorama.com/product/mark4-v3-pro-15)). No "Mark4 V3 10-inch" or "V4 10-inch" exists in any search result.

## Your 20 mm standoff pairs point to GEPRC geometry, not the 427 mm V2

The two STEP references already in hand give this baseline. The **GEPRC 7-inch** has standoff pairs of **31.4 / 20 / 20 / 19 mm** (front to rear) with inner arm holes at **20 and 36 mm**. The **RJX V2 10-inch (427 mm)** has **31.4 / 24 / 24 / 19 mm**. Your frame has a rear pair of about 19–20 mm, a second pair at 20 mm and a much wider front pair, which matches the GEPRC sequence. The 24 mm middle pairs that define the RJX/Readytosky/Tarot V2 cut do not appear. No public source gives standoff-pair or arm-bolt spacings as text for any 10-inch Mark4. That data exists only inside CAD files, and no CAD file could be downloaded in this session.

The **~24 mm inner arm-hole spacing does not fit either reference**. GEPRC's 7-inch uses 20 and 36 mm. Three explanations fit the evidence, in rough order of likelihood. First, a **GEPRC-geometry 10-inch**: GEPRC's own Mark4 10 or a clone that follows its outline, such as the UDC Mark4 10 (305 × 370 mm versus GEPRC's 303 × 376 mm). The wider 10-inch arm could carry a different bolt pattern from the 7-inch. Second, a **hybrid clone** with GEPRC-style standoff positions and its own arm cut. Third, an **aftermarket or locally cut 8 mm arm** drilled to its own pattern. That last option matters because the only 8 mm Mark4 10-inch arms found are the Rozetka fiberglass pair. They are sold for the 427 mm V2, so they presumably follow V2 bolt spacing, which sits awkwardly with your GEPRC-style 20 mm pairs. Note also that 24 mm equals the V2's standoff-pair width. That may be a coincidence, but checking the arm holes in the V2 STEP model would rule it in or out. An 8 mm thickness on your frame could also be a **7.5 mm V2-style arm read high** because of weave and coating, or a **fiberglass arm** (matte, grey-green laminate rather than twill carbon) cut thick to make up for lower stiffness.

Of the non-Mark4 8 mm frames, none is known to match your spacings. The Rekon10 Pro and XL10 V6 share the Mark4 idea of separate arms sandwiched in a three-plate stack with eight standoffs, but their geometries differ (455–474 mm stretched-X, and 420 mm True-X). Their standoff positions and diameters were not published in any source found.

## Calipers, a tape measure and two STEP downloads will settle it

Start by measuring the **motor-to-motor diagonal**. About **428–429 mm** points to the GEPRC family ([GetFPV](https://www.getfpv.com/geprc-mark4-10-frame-kit.html)), **427 mm** to the V2 clone, **420 mm** to the XL10 V6, **418 mm** to the Helion 10, and **455 or 474 mm** to the Rekon10 Pro V1 or V2. Then measure the **outline**. About **303–305 × 370–376 mm** is GEPRC/UDC geometry ([UDC](https://www.udc.net.ua/en/products/udc-mark4-10)), and about **268 × 332 mm** is the V2 ([Cults3D V2 model](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/gadget/fpv-frame-mark4-v2-10inch-deffinition)). Measure arm thickness with calipers at a bare cut edge away from coating, check whether the material is carbon twill or fiberglass, and note whether the motor mounts take 16 × 16, 19 × 19 or both. Then download the STEP models and measure their standoff pairs and arm-bolt holes against your numbers (19–20 / 20 / wide front, inner arm holes about 24 mm). For GEPRC geometry, use the GrabCAD "GEPRC Mark 4 Frame" model, whose 2024 update added full 5–10-inch frames, and the "GEPRC MK4 V2 10in" upload, which claims more accurate hole placement. Both were found through a user's likes page ([GrabCAD](https://grabcad.com/andy.lo-15/likes)), and there is a free mirror on [Marathon-OS](https://marathon-os.com/library/geprc-mk4-v2-10in-standard-mechanical-680dbef84366ea6baf6e9070). Further GEPRC models are on Cults3D: FlareFly's "Frame Mark 4 10 inch" STEP set ([Cults3D](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/gadget/frame-mark-4-10-inch-step-cad-files)), FPV_Lab's "GEPRC MK4 10in" ([Cults3D](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/gadget/geprc-mk4-10in)) and a 5–10-inch "GEPRC Mark 4" clone set ([Cults3D](https://cults3d.com/en/3d-model/gadget/geprc-mark-4)). There is also a model on [Thingiverse 4973650](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:4973650). For the V2, use the Cults3D "FPV frame Mark4 v2 10inch" (427 mm, 268.18 × 332.07 mm) linked above and the [CGTrader](https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/aircraft/other/fpv-frame-mark4-v2-10inch) model. Be aware that "GEPRC" on a V2 model is probably loose naming, because no GEPRC-branded 427 mm V2 was found. GEPRC's own downloads page has no frame CAD ([GEPRC downloads](https://geprc.com/downloads/mark4-series/)).

Before buying, open the EcoGalactics and Rozetka pages in a browser for photos, seller details and arm-bolt spacing. Ask any XL10 V6 seller to confirm 7 or 8 mm by caliper. Every figure in this report came from search-engine summaries, because direct page fetches (WebFetch and curl) were refused by the session's egress proxy for all the shops and CAD sites involved. Prices and stock were not live-checked, beyond what the search snippets showed in October 2026.

## Conclusion

An 8 mm carbon arm on a 10-inch Mark4 is almost certainly not a factory part. If your frame really has one, its origin is more informative than its brand. The most likely stories are a GEPRC-outline clone with locally cut thick arms (Ukraine has the most active local Mark4 10-inch scene in these results), a fiberglass arm mistaken for carbon, or a 7.5 mm V2 arm measuring high. The standoff evidence already narrows the search: 20 mm pairs rule out the dominant 427 mm V2 body, so the GEPRC-geometry STEP files are the first ones to check, not the V2 files.

If the goal is to buy rather than identify, there is a real trade-off. You can stay with true Mark4 geometry and accept 7–7.5 mm arms, or take a confirmed 8 mm frame (Rekon10 Pro V2 first, Helion 10 second) and accept a different layout. The third option is to get 8 mm arms cut from a downloaded Mark4 10 STEP file. Because the Mark4 arm is a separate plate clamped in a sandwich, that option is more practical than it sounds: the stack, plates and standoffs stay stock, and only the arm file changes.
